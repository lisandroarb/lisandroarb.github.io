            <a href="#about">I am a Chemical Oceanographer specialising in marine biogeochemistry, with a strong focus on the processes that regulate the global carbon cycle and the exchange of CO₂ between the ocean and the atmosphere. My research aims to improve our understanding of the mechanisms controlling ocean carbon uptake and variability, and how these processes interact with ocean sustainability and climate change.
My work particularly focuses on the Southern Ocean carbon sink, combining in-situ observations, observational synthesis products, and data-driven approaches to better constrain air–sea CO₂ fluxes and their variability. I am also interested in how observing systems can be optimised to more effectively monitor the ocean carbon cycle, especially in data-sparse regions such as the Southern Ocean.
More broadly, my research contributes to ongoing efforts to improve the design of ocean carbon observing networks, with the goal of developing observational strategies that are both scientifically robust and technologically feasible in the near term. This includes exploring how existing and emerging observing platforms can be integrated to better constrain surface and interior ocean carbon variability and support future observing system simulation experiments.
My approach to research is driven by a strong interest in combining observations, data analysis, and emerging methodologies to address key questions about the ocean’s role in the global climate system.</a>
