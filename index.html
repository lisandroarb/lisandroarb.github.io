About

I am an Argentine chemical oceanographer specialising in marine biogeochemistry, with a strong focus on the processes that regulate the global carbon cycle and the exchange of CO₂ between the ocean and the atmosphere.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at LOCEAN-CNRS in Paris, France, within the European project TRICUSO. My work focuses on optimising the ocean CO₂ observing system in the Southern Ocean, combining the SOCAT database with autonomous BGC-Argo floats to better estimate the Southern Ocean carbon sink and reduce its uncertainties.

In parallel, I continue collecting and working with surface ocean pCO₂ data from the Argentine continental shelf, through the underway system on board R/V Víctor Angelescu (INIDEP). I process and quality-control these observations, submit them to SOCAT, and use them to study air-sea CO₂ fluxes in the Southwest Atlantic.

Before that, during my PhD, I studied the drivers of air-sea CO₂ fluxes and carbonate system variability in the Drake Passage and the northern Antarctic Peninsula.

Projects

My work spans from the open Southern Ocean to the Argentine continental shelf and coastal estuaries, including the scientific baselines needed for marine carbon dioxide removal (mCDR). These are the projects I am currently involved in.

Southern OceanObserving systems

TRICUSO - Three Research Infrastructures together: Carbon Uptake Southern Ocean

Horizon Europe (Grant No. 101188028) · 2025 - 2028

A large European effort to better quantify how much carbon the Southern Ocean absorbs, combining ship-based observations, autonomous floats and data products.

PI: Dr. Richard Sanders (NORCE)
My role: postdoctoral researcher in WP3 (Observing system design and strategy, led by VLIZ) and WP5 (Optimisation of the Southern Ocean carbon observing system, led by NORCE).
mCDRSouthwest Atlantic

Establishing baseline conditions for responsible mCDR deployment in the Argentine Southwestern Atlantic

ClimateWorks Foundation · 2026 - 2028

Builds the observational baseline of the carbonate system and air-sea CO₂ exchange needed before marine carbon dioxide removal can be evaluated responsibly in Argentine waters.

PI: Dr. Carla F. Berghoff (INIDEP)
My role: mainly involved in WP1 (Air-sea CO₂ fluxes and carbonate system pattern recognition), and partially in WP2 (Physical drivers of CO₂ variability) and WP4 (Ocean literacy and stakeholder co-design).
Continental shelfCarbon cycle

Carbon uptake mechanisms and fate on the northern Argentine continental shelf

CONICET PIP 11220250101386CO · 2026 - 2028

Investigates the processes that drive CO₂ uptake on the northern Argentine shelf and what happens to that carbon once it enters the ocean.

PI: Dr. Celeste López-Abbate (IADO)
My role: quantifying net air-sea CO₂ fluxes on the shelf and attributing their variability to physical drivers (water masses, stratification, wind and vertical mixing) and biological drivers (blooms and plankton activity).
EstuaryCoastal carbon

Smart-Port Net-Zero Challenge: natural decarbonisation potential of the Bahía Blanca Estuary

Port of Bahía Blanca · 2023 - Present

Characterises the carbonate system and air-sea CO₂ exchange in the Bahía Blanca Estuary to identify CO₂ sink and source areas, providing a baseline to guide nature-based negative-emission tools in this coastal environment.

PIs: Dr. Celeste López-Abbate, Dr. Ana L. Delgado (IADO)
My role: carbonate system measurements and the management and analysis of air-sea CO₂ flux and carbonate system data.
